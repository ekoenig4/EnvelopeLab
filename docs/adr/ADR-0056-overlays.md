# ADR-0056: Overlays: appliqués sewn on the envelope (project format 8)

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-20

## Context
Builders sew flat patches on top of the envelope: logos, eyes, stars, stripes. They can
lie anywhere and across seams, and need a cut piece and the line to sew them on, marked
on every panel they cross. Special shapes (ADR-0044) are inflated appendages with skins
and simulations; an appliqué is none of that.

## Decision
* A separate object type, `OverlaySpec` (`envelopelab.project.overlays`): name (unique
  among shapes and overlays), placement (`ShapePlacement`: gore, tape position, across),
  outline (circle, ellipse, rectangle, polygon with optional smoothing, or an outline
  imported from DXF/SVG through `envelopelab.io.outline_file`), width, height, turn, hem,
  optional fabric. `DesignState.overlays`, written in project format 8 (not yet
  released) only when there are any; older files read as having none.
* Geometry in cloth coordinates (`docs/theory/overlays.md`): exact in each flat panel's
  pattern coordinates, so the cut piece and the marking lines come from the same numbers;
  the ease (piece outline less marked lines) is reported, with a warning above 1 %.
* The input group `overlays` (optional) is read only by the export: an overlay edit makes
  a written build pack stale, never patterns, meshes, simulations or shapes.
* Colours: an overlay is coloured as a one-piece shape, `PanelRef(name, "A", 1)`
  (ADR-0055), so the colour picker, the Colours grid (overlays drawn and painted over the
  panels; fill tools never paint them), the 3D views and the build pack's colour lines
  treat it like any piece.
* Edits `add_overlay`, `update_overlay` (refused when the overlay would not fit),
  `remove_overlay`, `duplicate_overlay`, one undo step each through
  `ProjectSession.edit_overlays`; every other edit keeps the overlays.
* The Special shapes mode lists overlays with the shapes (**Add shape ▸ Overlay
  (appliqué)**); selecting one shows an overlay editor (placement, outline, size, turn,
  corners or import, hem, fabric, colour, the cut piece drawn with what it covers and its
  fit). A polygon's corners are dragged in that drawing (double click an edge to add
  one, double click or right click one to remove it; a self-crossing outline is refused
  while dragging, one undo step per drag); the corner table is folded away for exact
  values. A polygon may have mirror axes (`symmetry`, 0 to 12, the first up the tape):
  only the corners of one slice between the first two axes are stored and the outline
  is generated by the dihedral group (`symmetric_polygon`), so it is symmetric by
  construction; the editor maps a drag, an added or a removed corner back to the slice.
  A scale dial scales the shape about its centre (live, one undo step per turn). Overlays are drawn on the gore map and in 3D and are dragged and nudged like
  shapes. The 3D / Simulation view has a layer per overlay. The Validation panel reports
  an overlay that cannot be laid (error) or fits badly (warning).
* Build pack (`envelopelab.export.overlays`): per overlay a piece sheet (kind `overlay`,
  cut 1, `<slug>-<name>-overlay.pdf`), a marking sheet per panel its outline crosses
  (named like a shape's, so a panel's marking template draws every shape's and overlay's
  lines), numbered match marks at every seam crossing, and a seam pair with the planned
  ease, checked by the output QA. The colour plan draws the overlays over the gores.

## Consequences
* Overlays are not mirrored by symmetry axes or grouped with shapes yet.
* The marking lines follow the flat panels; a large patch on a strongly curved part of
  the envelope gets a fit warning rather than a doubly curved development.
* Overlays do not change the envelope's mass estimate or simulations (an appliqué adds a
  little cloth; its weight is not modelled).
